# 0008: Context size is the cost driver, not effort

Date: 2026-09-06, revised 2026-09-07. Status: accepted. Measured on one laptop: 377 `.jsonl` files under `~/.claude/projects`, of which 329 are subagent transcripts and 9 of the remaining 48 hold only queue-operation records, leaving **39 real sessions**; 35,527 records; and 1,087 Codex rollouts. An earlier revision of this record said "367 transcripts", which counted subagent files as sessions.

- Both tools record an effort or reasoning level, and the obvious feature is to manage it. The measurement says that is the wrong lever.
- Effort moves output tokens by about 2.5x: over 10,134 Claude assistant records carrying the field, mean output was 1,010 tokens at `medium` and 2,491 at `max`.
- Cache read is about 300x the output count and does not track effort at all. It tracks how much context the model re-reads, which is a function of session length. `xhigh` showed a higher mean cache read than `max`.
- Within a session the level climbs near-monotonically, with occasional drops where compaction ran. Across 33 sessions of 50 or more replies: floor 32,234 tokens (median of the first five replies), mean 211,619, peak 375,041 (median). **A long session's context peaks at roughly 12x its opening level.** That is a claim about tokens.
- In dollars the same growth is a **1.69x** multiple on the session, not 12x, because the tokens it adds are cache reads and cache reads are priced far below fresh input. An earlier revision of this record read "roughly 17x per reply" without saying tokens, which invited it to be read as a cost. Two later attempts to put a dollar figure on it returned 1.28x and 1.89x; both were wrong, the second because it priced every cache write at the 5-minute rate. Growth is also sub-linear, not quadratic: measured, 34.7% of a session's cache-read cost is spent by the halfway point, where a quadratic predicts 12.5%.
- Decision: context is the headline. `sessions` reports peak input tokens and peak per assistant reply, above the effort row. Per reply, not per human turn: fan-out runs from under 6 to over 30 replies per turn, so a per-turn figure measures unattended running rather than context. Effort ships as a mix of labels and is never rendered as a cost.
- Peak, not last or mean. Peak is a max, so it survives both the compaction drops and the zeroed records that stand in for a reply that never came. A mean is dragged by both, and last is zero on a session that ended on an error.
- Nothing here is priced. Multiplying tokens by a rate inside a reader is forbidden (`docs/adding-a-provider.md`), and the ratios above are one machine's measurement, not a published fact, so they stay in this record rather than in output.
- The contrast between providers is left to the reader of each section: on this laptop Claude peaked at a median 304k per reply against Codex at 14k. Those are different tools with different context windows and the report never puts them in one sentence.
